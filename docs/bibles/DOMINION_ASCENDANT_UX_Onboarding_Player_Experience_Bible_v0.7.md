# DOMINION // ASCENDANT
## UX, Onboarding & Player Experience Bible — v0.7

**Status:** Canonical player-experience pass following:

1. Game Bible v0.1
2. Playable Systems Bible v0.2
3. Combat, Siege & Conquest Bible v0.3
4. Citizen, Card Acquisition & World Campaign Bible v0.4
5. 3D Production & Technical Architecture Bible v0.5
6. Content Production Bible v0.6

> **Teach the player by letting the city become more complicated only after they understand why the next system matters.**

---

# 1. PURPOSE

DOMINION // ASCENDANT combines:

- TCG deckbuilding
- persistent 3D city building
- economic simulation
- citizen simulation
- RPG exploration
- tactical combat
- diplomacy
- conquest
- world strategy

That combination is the game's strength and its greatest onboarding risk.

This document defines how the game teaches those systems without burying the player under:

- popups,
- codex walls,
- mandatory videos,
- tutorial menus,
- unexplained numbers.

The player should understand the game through **cause → consequence → response**.

---

# 2. ONBOARDING PRINCIPLE

The player does not need to understand everything before playing.

They only need to understand:

> **What matters right now?**

The game reveals systems in layers.

### Layer 1
Place a card.

### Layer 2
Make the building work.

### Layer 3
Give people somewhere to live.

### Layer 4
Give them jobs.

### Layer 5
Balance the city.

### Layer 6
Walk through what you built.

### Layer 7
Meet another civilization.

### Layer 8
Make a choice that affects them.

### Layer 9
Fight or negotiate.

### Layer 10
Conquer and absorb.

---

# 3. THE FIRST 60 MINUTES

The first hour is divided into 12 five-minute experience blocks.

The exact time may vary by player, but the pacing target matters.

---

# MINUTE 0–5
# ARRIVAL

## Player Experience

Opening cinematic.

The Founder arrives at a dormant urban foundation site in the Synara frontier.

Only the Founder Hall exists.

The city is quiet.

No economy UI is visible yet.

The player controls the Founder in third person.

Objectives:

- walk to Founder Hall,
- inspect the central city model,
- activate the city core.

## Systems Introduced

- Founder movement
- camera
- interact
- physical scale
- Founder Hall

## Narrative Goal

The player understands:

> **This place is mine to build.**

No deck tutorial yet.

---

# MINUTE 5–10
# THE FIRST CARD

Inside Founder Hall, the player finds the starter deck.

The interface presents one card:

# ADAPTIVE HABITAT

The player inspects it.

The card rotates into a 3D building preview.

The interface transitions to City Mode.

The player sees a limited buildable district.

## Tutorial Prompt

**Place your first Habitat.**

Only valid cells are highlighted.

The player confirms placement.

The card physically transitions into the construction site.

The building completes.

## Systems Introduced

- Card inspection
- City Mode
- grid placement
- footprint
- construction animation

## Emotional Goal

The player should immediately understand:

> **The card became that building.**

This is the game's first "aha" moment.

---

# MINUTE 10–15
# PEOPLE ARRIVE

Residents move into the Habitat.

The player returns to Founder Mode.

A small group physically arrives.

One is:

# NIA VALE

The player can speak with her.

Nia comments that the building has power but needs better city services.

## Systems Introduced

- citizens
- named citizens
- population
- housing
- city inspection

The player receives:

- Microgrid Station
- Water Reclaimer

The game teaches infrastructure through an actual need.

---

# MINUTE 15–20
# MAKE IT WORK

The player places:

- Microgrid Station
- Water Reclaimer

Utility overlays briefly appear.

The Habitat changes from:

**Partially Supplied**
to
**Operational**

Residents react.

The city gains its first stable production.

## Systems Introduced

- power
- water
- utility radius
- operational state
- infrastructure cards

No data network yet.

---

# MINUTE 20–25
# WORK AND ECONOMY

Nia asks for employment.

The player receives:

- Cognitive Operations Tower
- Corner Exchange

The player places both.

A small hiring panel shows:

**Jobs Created: 7**

Nearby citizens begin taking jobs.

The Corner Exchange begins producing Capital.

## Systems Introduced

- jobs
- workforce
- Capital
- building production
- staffing

Only Capital is explained at this stage.

Insight and Influence remain hidden or minimized.

---

# MINUTE 25–30
# THE FIRST DECISION

The player receives:

# AUTONOMOUS EXCHANGE

It requires almost no workers.

The game explicitly compares:

### Corner Exchange
More workers.
Moderate output.

### Autonomous Exchange
Few workers.
Higher efficiency.

The player chooses whether to build it.

If built:

Synara's **Dependency** meter becomes visible for the first time.

Nia reacts.

## Systems Introduced

- faction special mechanic
- systemic problem
- tradeoff
- city metric warning

The player learns:

> Strength creates consequence.

---

# MINUTE 30–35
# THE CITY BECOMES A PLACE

A short free-exploration window opens.

The player can:

- walk through buildings,
- talk to residents,
- inspect street activity,
- see autonomous services,
- climb to a rooftop overlook.

Nia's optional dialogue expands.

The player may discover:

- a worker complaint,
- a scenic overlook,
- a small loot/cache location.

## Systems Introduced

- exploration
- side content
- citizen stories
- Discovery Cache

This is deliberately a lower-pressure phase.

---

# MINUTE 35–40
# THE SECOND CURRENCY

Nia sends the player to the Cognitive Operations Tower.

A research opportunity appears:

# NETWORK OPTIMIZATION

Completing it generates:

# INSIGHT

Insight appears in the HUD.

The player spends Insight to upgrade the Neural Relay blueprint.

## Systems Introduced

- Insight
- research
- blueprint
- upgrade
- card crafting

Capital remains construction/economy.

Insight is framed as knowledge.

---

# MINUTE 40–45
# THE FIRST FACTION TENSION

Synara's Human Agency League sends a petition:

> Automation is expanding faster than citizen oversight.

The player sees two responses:

### Build Agency Forum
Addresses the structural cause.

### Ignore
No immediate cost.

No arbitrary “Spend 5 Influence to make protest vanish.”

If ignored, tension grows.

If addressed, citizens react.

## Systems Introduced

- faction
- grievance
- civic response
- Agency
- Dependency

Influence is introduced as a political resource.

---

# MINUTE 45–50
# CONTACT

A transmission arrives from Forgeweave.

Daxton Rhe appears through live communication.

He requests machine components.

At the same time, Eden Circuit warns that Forgeweave's extraction is damaging the basin.

The player is given three initial diplomatic responses:

- trade with Forgeweave,
- hear Eden's proposal,
- remain neutral.

## Systems Introduced

- world map
- diplomacy
- rival civilizations
- trade
- relationship consequences

The world opens beyond the city.

---

# MINUTE 50–55
# THE FRONTIER

The Founder travels to a nearby neutral resource site.

The player encounters:

- Survey objective
- minor environmental hazard
- first hostile scavenger/drone group

The player receives a basic combat tutorial.

## Systems Introduced

- Founder combat
- Guard
- Stamina
- one active ability
- loot/discovery
- resource site

Combat is introduced only after the player cares about the world.

---

# MINUTE 55–60
# COMMAND

A Synara Guardian Drone Cohort arrives.

The player enters Command Mode for the first time.

Tutorial:

- select squad
- move
- hold
- attack
- use cover

The player and squad capture the site.

After victory:

Forgeweave claims the resource.

Eden claims the extraction would damage the basin.

The first major world decision appears.

## Systems Introduced

- Command Mode
- squad control
- cover
- tactical objective
- contested territory

## End-of-Hour Hook

The player now understands that:

> **Building the city, developing people, collecting cards, diplomacy, and combat are all parts of the same world.**

---

# 4. FIRST SESSION RULE

Do not introduce in the first hour:

- advanced multiclassing
- global market simulation
- Dominion Doctrine loadouts
- multi-front war
- governance modes
- complex espionage
- full card mastery
- deep faction coalition mechanics
- hybrid districts
- Axiom endgame systems

The player can see hints that these systems exist.

They do not need to operate them yet.

# 6. TUTORIAL DESIGN RULES

## RULE 1 — TEACH THROUGH NEED

Do not say:

> Water is a city utility.

Instead:

The Habitat runs out of water.

Then give the player the tool that solves it.

---

## RULE 2 — ONE NEW PRIMARY IDEA AT A TIME

Do not introduce:

- water,
- power,
- network,
- staffing,
- adjacency

in one screen.

Layer them.

---

## RULE 3 — THE PLAYER SHOULD ACT WITHIN 15 SECONDS

Tutorial text should immediately lead to a meaningful action.

---

## RULE 4 — NEVER BLOCK EXPERT PLAYERS UNNECESSARILY

Experienced players can:

- skip explanatory dialogue,
- disable tutorial callouts,
- use keyboard/controller shortcuts immediately.

Mandatory story beats remain.

# 7. CONTEXTUAL TEACHING

The game uses:

### MICRO PROMPTS
One-line contextual guidance.

### HIGHLIGHTING
Show relevant world objects.

### GHOST PREVIEW
Preview effect before confirmation.

### CAUSE CARDS
Explain why a metric changed.

### CHARACTER DIALOGUE
NPCs explain systems naturally.

### OPTIONAL CODEX
Deep detail for players who want it.

---

# 8. CAUSE CARDS

When a value changes significantly:

Example:

# HAPPINESS -8

Cause card:

- -4 Long Commute
- -3 Automation Anxiety
- -1 Industrial Noise

The player can inspect each cause.

This is preferable to forcing them through opaque spreadsheets.

---

# 9. ALERT PRIORITY

Alerts have four levels.

## INFO
No action required.

## ATTENTION
Worth reviewing.

## WARNING
Meaningful negative trajectory.

## CRISIS
Immediate action likely needed.

Only Crisis interrupts gameplay with strong presentation.

---

# 10. ALERT BUDGET

Never show more than:

- 1 Crisis alert
- 2 Warning alerts
- 4 Attention items

at once.

Additional items enter an inbox.

This prevents dashboard overload.

---

- Build

Selected card/building information.

Hand / build queue.

# 12. CITY CARD HAND

The hand should feel like a strategic tray, not a mobile-game bar.

Cards show:

- art
- cost
- type
- footprint
- readiness

Hover/select expands the card.

Unavailable cards explain exactly why:

- insufficient Capital
- no valid terrain
- blueprint locked
- insufficient power capacity
- hand restriction

# 13. BUILD PLACEMENT UX

When a card is selected:

## GREEN
Valid.

## AMBER
Valid but has a meaningful downside.

Example:
- pollution near housing.

## RED
Invalid.

The preview displays:

- footprint
- road requirement
- utilities
- positive adjacency
- negative adjacency
- district effect

Before confirmation.

---

# 14. PLACEMENT CONSEQUENCE PREVIEW

Example:

Placing Synthetic Fabrication Node:

**Effects**
- +6 Production
- +4 Jobs
- +1 Industrial Density

**Nearby**
- Adaptive Habitat: -4 Health
- Neural Relay: +8% efficiency

The game teaches planning before regret.

The deck builder has three main areas:

## COLLECTION

## ACTIVE DECK

60-card deck.

## ANALYSIS

Shows:

- card-type distribution
- cost curve
- faction composition
- utility coverage
- housing potential
- economic potential
- combat potential
- copy-limit violations

- Civilization

- Rarity
- Cost
- Footprint
- Tags
- Upgrade

- Acquired
- Favorite

# 18. DECK WARNINGS

Warnings should be strategic rather than prescriptive.

Example:

**Low Residential Capacity**
Your deck may struggle to support enough workers.

**High Utility Demand**
Your current deck consumes substantially more power than it can generate.

**Heavy Foreign Composition**
Only 30 founding-civilization cards remain.

Do not claim there is one “correct” deck.

---

# 19. COLLECTION UX

Card collection view should support:

- card-grid view
- civilization view
- card-type view
- 3D asset view
- history view

Selecting a card can transition from card art into the actual 3D asset.

---

# 20. BLUEPRINT UX

Blueprints are visually distinct from owned cards.

Blueprint view tells:

- what the card does
- crafting cost
- required facility
- number owned
- eligible decks

Craft button remains contextual.

---

# 21. DISCOVERY CACHE EXPERIENCE

The reward experience should feel satisfying but not manipulative.

Sequence:

1. Cache appears physically.
2. Player opens it.
3. contents display clearly.
4. new items are identified.
5. duplicates show recycle/fuse options.
6. pity/protection systems remain invisible unless inspected.

No casino-style near-miss effects.

---

# 22. FOUNDER HUD

Default Founder HUD should remain minimal.

## LEFT
Health / Guard.

## BOTTOM CENTER
Abilities.

## RIGHT
Current contextual tool/ammo.

## TOP
Objective when relevant.

City currencies fade unless changed or relevant.

---

# 23. FOUNDER INTERACTION UX

Looking at an interactable shows:

- name
- interaction
- relevant state

Example:

**Nia Vale**
Talk

or:

**Synthetic Fabrication Node**
Operational
Inspect

No giant floating panels over every object.

# 24. COMMAND MODE UX

Command Mode should make tactical complexity readable in 2–3 seconds.

Primary display:

- squads
- control zones
- supply
- current objective
- enemy known positions
- CP

Important geometry becomes subtly emphasized.

# 25. COMMAND SELECTION

Controller:

- radial squad selector
- cycle units
- cursor target

Mouse:
- click
- drag-select where appropriate
- hotkey groups

Both must have parity.

# 26. COMMAND ORDER LANGUAGE

Core orders:

- Move
- Attack
- Hold
- Defend
- Capture
- Escort
- Retreat
- Ability

Faction-specific orders appear contextually.

---

# 27. COMMAND PATH PREVIEW

Before confirming Move:

show:

- route
- estimated travel
- cover exposure
- supply effect
- danger if known

This keeps tactical play understandable.

---

# 28. COMBAT TELEGRAPHS

Threats use:

- geometry
- animation
- sound
- iconography

not color alone.

Area attack:
- ground boundary
- directional warning
- sound cue
- animation wind-up.

---

# 29. DAMAGE FEEDBACK

Player should understand:

- who was hit
- by what
- whether shield/armor absorbed it
- major status effect

Avoid excessive floating numbers.

Numbers may be available in tactical accessibility setting.

---

# 30. CIVILIAN SAFETY UX

When civilian risk becomes important:

the game marks:

- civilian concentration
- evacuation routes
- dangerous structures

Rules of Engagement remain visible during siege.

The player should never accidentally discover after the battle that an opaque system considered them reckless.

---

# 31. DIPLOMACY SCREEN

Diplomacy is organized around a **relationship portrait**, not a spreadsheet.

Center:

Leader and civilization.

Primary values:

- Trust
- Respect
- Fear
- Dependence
- Grievance
- Compatibility

Each value can be expanded into reasons.

---

# 32. DIPLOMATIC MEMORY

Example:

**Trust 61**

Reasons:
- +15 Delivered coolant during shortage
- +9 Protected Forgeweave workers
- -8 Spied on freight network
- -5 Supported Eden restrictions

This makes diplomacy legible.

---

# 33. TREATY BUILDER

Treaties are constructed from clauses.

Example:

# INDUSTRIAL RESOURCE COMPACT

Forgeweave receives:
- 10 machine components / World Tick

Synara receives:
- 4 Capital
- 1 Forgeweave Blueprint progress

Duration:
10 World Ticks

The player sees real consequences.

---

# 34. WORLD MAP UX

The World Map has layers:

## POLITICAL
Borders, relations.

## ECONOMIC
Trade, resource flow.

## MILITARY
Armies, conflict zones.

## CIVIC
Migration, instability.

## AXIOM
Relics, strange activity.

The player chooses one layer at a time.

---

# 35. WORLD MAP CAMERA

The map should be an actual stylized 3D strategic globe/terrain view where practical.

Cities rise physically from regions.

Trade routes animate subtly.

War zones show activity.

Avoid cluttering with hundreds of icons.

---

# 36. EVENT PRESENTATION

Events appear as:

- world change
- character reaction
- concise event card
- available choices

Example:

# THE FOUNDRY SHORTAGE

**Machine components have risen 38%.**

Daxton requests emergency supply.

Amara warns further extraction will damage the basin.

Choices:
- Support Forgeweave
- Support Eden
- Broker
- Exploit market
- Delay response

---

# 37. EVENT CONSEQUENCE PREVIEW

Choices show:

### KNOWN EFFECTS

What the Founder can reasonably predict.

### POSSIBLE RISKS

Uncertain consequences.

Do not reveal every hidden outcome.

---

# 38. FACTION UX

Faction screen shows:

- support
- approval
- organization
- radicalization
- top demand
- leader
- recent actions

No giant ideological skill tree.

# 39. FACTION DEMAND UX

Example:

# HUMAN AGENCY LEAGUE

- Build Agency Forum

# 41. IMPORTANT CITIZEN PINNING

The player may pin citizens.

- promotions
- crises
- faction changes
- Champion eligibility

# 42. FIRST NIA VALE ONBOARDING ARC

Nia acts as a human anchor for the first session.

She introduces:

1. housing
2. jobs
3. automation tension
4. research
5. factions
6. citizen agency

Her dialogue changes according to how much the player automates.

If the player heavily automates:

Nia becomes more skeptical.

If balanced:

she becomes collaborative.

If human-first:

she may challenge whether the city is ignoring useful technology.

She should not simply agree with the player.

---

# 43. FAILURE RECOVERY

The game should rarely answer failure with:

**Reload Save**

Instead:

## Economic Failure
Emergency credit / reduced construction / political consequences.

## Housing Failure
Overcrowding / migration / temporary shelter.

## Combat Failure
Retreat / captured units / lost objective.

## Diplomatic Failure
Worse relationship / alternate route.

## Faction Failure
protest / strike / political escalation.

Failure creates gameplay.

---

# 44. SOFT FAILURE

Most systems use escalating failure.

Example:

Power:

1. strain
2. rolling outages
3. critical blackout
4. infrastructure damage

The player gets time to respond.

---

# 45. HARD FAILURE

Hard failure should be reserved for:

- Founder death in Iron Dominion modes
- total loss of specific story objective
- final endgame condition

Even campaign defeat should ideally offer a recovery branch where possible.

---

# 46. AUTOSAVE PRESENTATION

Autosave is subtle.

The player sees a small icon.

Major save moments:
- after Ascension
- before irreversible Leader decision

can show:

**Campaign State Secured**

Avoid breaking immersion.

---

# 47. MANUAL SAVE

Allow:

- manual named saves
- quick save where difficulty permits
- multiple campaign slots

Do not artificially restrict standard single-player saves.

---

# 50. ACCESSIBILITY — COGNITIVE

Support:

- simplified city alerts

# 51. ACCESSIBILITY — STRATEGIC ASSIST

Optional assist modes:

## Utility Assist
Automatically suggests utility fixes.

## Workforce Assist
Suggests staffing/housing changes.

## Governor Assist
Automates mature districts.

## Combat Command Assist
Squads use smarter autonomy.

These reduce complexity without removing systems.

---

# 52. CONTROLLER NAVIGATION

The game must be controller-complete.

Global rules:

- shoulder buttons cycle major panels
- radial menus for dense actions
- D-pad for common mode-specific actions
- consistent Confirm/Back behavior
- focus never gets trapped

---

# 53. MODE SWITCH UX

Default:

Founder → City:
single dedicated input.

Founder → Command:
single dedicated input during combat.

City → Founder:
focus Founder marker and descend.

City → World:
open strategic map.

Transitions visually connect the spaces.

---

# 54. UI TRANSITION LANGUAGE

UI transforms rather than hard cuts.

Example:

Founder minimap
→ expands upward
→ becomes tactical map
→ camera rises into Command Mode.

This reinforces one-world continuity.

---

# 55. FIRST CONQUEST ONBOARDING

Do not expose all four conquest meters immediately.

The player first experiences them through Forgeweave.

When Forgeweave conflict begins, introduce:

### Military Sovereignty

If player pursues trade:
reveal Economic Autonomy.

If player engages worker factions:
reveal Civic Legitimacy.

If player builds trust:
reveal Alliance Readiness.

The UI follows player behavior.

---

# 56. CONQUEST DASHBOARD

Once unlocked:

shows four routes.

Each displays:

- current progress
- why it changed
- next known opportunities
- risks

Example:

# ECONOMIC AUTONOMY — 58

Pressure:
- 40% freight controlled
- emergency credit accepted

Resistance:
- alternative supplier contract
- national reserve

---

# 57. PLAYER SHOULD NEVER FEEL LOCKED INTO ONE ROUTE

A Force player can negotiate.

An Alliance player can respond militarily.

An Economic player can support worker movements.

The dashboard shows mixed progress.

---

# 58. LEADER ENCOUNTER UX

Before final confrontation:

summarize history.

Example:

# DAXTON RHE

Respect: 72  
Trust: 48  
Grievance: 31

**He remembers**
- you preserved worker housing
- you seized the Freight Corridor
- you helped contain the Foundry Shortage
- your spies breached his supply network

This makes the boss/negotiation feel earned.

---

# 59. BOSS HUD

Leader encounters should not use a generic giant health bar alone.

Show:

- Leader state
- critical system
- phase objective

Example:

**DAXTON RHE — INDUSTRIAL OVERDRIVE**

Grand Forge Heat: 78%  
Coolant Stability: 42%  
Military Sovereignty: 16

Objective:
**Disable or stabilize the Overdrive system.**

---

# 60. ASCENSION PRESENTATION

Ascension should temporarily simplify all UI.

At climax:

HUD fades.

World takes priority.

Only essential prompts remain.

After Relic transfer:

unlock summary appears in a deliberate sequence.

---

# 61. ASCENSION REWARD SCREEN

Do not dump 15 rewards at once.

Reveal:

### 1 — RELIC
Forge Relic acquired.

### 2 — DOMINION DOCTRINE
Replication.

### 3 — CIVILIZATION CARDS
Forgeweave pool unlocked.

### 4 — CLASS LINEAGE
Forgeweave classes.

### 5 — LEADER
Daxton outcome.

### 6 — FUSION DISCOVERY
Autonomous Factory.

Then:
**Return to Capital.**

---

# 62. RETURN HOME AFTER ASCENSION

The player should physically see:

- new architecture
- citizens discussing Forgeweave
- new faction reactions
- Founder Hall transformation
- new construction options
- new music layer

The reward should exist in the city, not only in menus.

---

# 63. FIRST 3 HOURS PACING

## Hour 1
Build and discover.

## Hour 2
Diplomacy, citizens, exploration, first meaningful combat.

## Hour 3
Foundry Shortage escalates; player chooses emerging Forgeweave strategy.

No conquest should be completed before the player understands what they are absorbing.

---

# 64. FIRST 10 HOURS PACING

Suggested:

### Hours 0–2
Synara foundation.

### 2–4
Forgeweave/Eden regional politics.

### 4–6
First serious crisis and card expansion.

### 6–8
Forgeweave conflict escalates.

### 8–10
First Ascension.

The exact pacing can be accelerated through difficulty/session settings.

# 104. ONBOARDING SUCCESS GATE

Do not add more civilizations to the tutorial until a new player can complete the first hour without developer explanation.

Observe silently.

If the player repeatedly asks:

> “What am I supposed to do?”

the UX failed.

If they ask:

> “What happens if I do this instead?”

the UX succeeded.

That distinction is fundamental.
